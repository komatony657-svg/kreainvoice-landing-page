What makes a good creative invoice template?

Most invoice templates are built for accountants, not creatives. They're functional but generic — rows of numbers with no visual identity. A good template for creative work does more:

  • Reflects your brand. Your logo, your colours, your fonts. An invoice is a client touchpoint and it should feel like your studio.
  • Handles usage rights. Photographers, videographers, and illustrators need to specify licensing terms on the invoice itself. Generic templates don't have this section.
  • Works across currencies. If you invoice clients internationally — or in UGX, KES, NGN — the template needs proper currency support, not just a dollar sign.
  • Exports a clean PDF. The PDF is what the client actually sees. It should look exactly like the preview.
  • Doesn't require a monthly subscription. You shouldn't pay a recurring fee just to send an invoice.

Tips for a professional-looking invoice

Use a consistent invoice number format

Kreainvoice auto-increments after each PDF download. Use a prefix that means something — your initials, your studio code, the year. KS-2026-001 looks more intentional than just 1.

Always specify payment terms clearly

Don't assume "net 30" is understood. Write it explicitly in your payment notes: "Payment due within 14 days of invoice date via MTN Mobile Money: 0771234567." The clearer the instruction, the faster you get paid.

Itemise even when it feels unnecessary

Three separate line items for photography, editing, and usage rights communicates value far better than one line that says "Photography — $500". Clients who can see the breakdown are less likely to push back on the total.

Include usage rights even on simple jobs

Even if you're being generous with licensing, stating it explicitly protects you. "Web use, Uganda only, 12 months" is a specific thing you agreed to. "Usage included" is a dispute waiting to happen.
